Compute sharing / In development

Now you’re the ISP

Your AI compute, shared on your terms.

ISP here means AI inference provision, not internet connectivity.

AIPSTER is being built to help you securely share AI inference with people you choose, from your own machines or supported providers, with control over access, limits, and revocation.

Planned support spans local CPUs, GPUs and clusters, plus eligible API-backed capacity. This site presents the direction; secure compute sharing is not live yet.

The direction

Capacity you own.
Access you decide.

The goal is to help you make AI inference capacity available to the people and teams you authorize, with clear permissions and visibility into how it is used.

API-backed capacity is in scope only where provider terms permit sharing or resale. Provider credentials stay private; arbitrary subscription resale is not part of this proposal.

01 / Permissions

Choose your circle

The intended design lets owners grant access to selected people or teams, with explicit permissions and revocation.

02 / Control

Keep limits visible

Planned usage visibility and owner-set limits put control over capacity and access in the owner’s hands.

03 / Roadmap

Explore earning later

Monetization is a future possibility for eligible capacity, subject to provider terms. A marketplace and billing are not live.

01 / Underlying research

From bounded search
to a separate release decision.

The underlying research combines IRB refinement infrastructure, an external Darwin Gödel Machine (DGM) controller, bounded experiment loops and optional JEV advice. The proposed inference fabric routes whole workloads across machines rather than splitting a model across them.

The figure retains its published conceptual labels. Those labels are not asserted to be a canonical match to current inference-fabric repository naming.

02 / Model & LoRA

Resident base weights.
Serial adapter switching.

The proposed runtime selects compatible, versioned LoRA adapters through a serial, request-safe switching sequence. Base weights remain immutable within each trial.
